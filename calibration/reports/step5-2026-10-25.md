## Where the labels came from

- Pairs: 30 hand-written traps plus 58 real pairs (round 1), 25 synonym-candidate traps plus 20 real pairs
  (round 2, added because round 1 had too few `same` pairs), and 41 real pairs (round 3, all holdout, added
  to make the holdout test bigger). Every split was assigned before any cosine was computed; real pairs
  came from the blind `calibrate --suggest` sampler.
- The owner labelled rounds 1 and 2. Then three LLM reviewers relabelled all 174 pairs against the written
  definitions, starting from the owner's answers, and the final label is their majority. They changed 10 of
  the owner's 133 labels and labelled round 3. The owner reviewed and accepted the final list.
- So the thresholds reflect the owner's judgement as reviewed by three models, not the owner alone.

## Decision (owner, 2026-10-25)

- ADJ = 0.68 is accepted: holdout F1 0.82 on 87 holdout pairs passes the 0.80 gate.
- HAVE = 0.90 is accepted with its weakness recorded. Holdout F1 is 0.50 (tune 0.52): with bare skill names,
  `same` and `related` pairs share the 0.84-0.97 cosine range, so no single cut separates them. More labels
  cannot fix that.
- The S5.3 fallback (context-enriched embeddings) is deferred, not dropped. HAVE reaches the score only
  through `cover_bar`, which defaults to the midpoint of ADJ and HAVE and is tuned against the owner's job
  ratings in Step 7. S5.3 is revisited if Step 7 shows the ranking is off.
- Known ADJ misses worth watching in Step 7: some sibling pairs the labels call related sit below 0.68
  (fastapi / django 0.62, node.js / express 0.61, redis / caching 0.67, rag / vector databases 0.55), so they
  earn no partial credit on their own.
